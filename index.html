Joynal Ali

Language is so embedded into the ordinary life that its feat of computation is often unnoticed- a silent architecture the mind/brain builds and processes before even the utterance ends.

My research focuses on the interaction of syntax with discourse. I am particularly interested in discourse particles, allocutive & phi-feature agreements, the functional projection HonP, negation, the tree-top structure, and the linguistic phenomena that offer a window into the architecture of the left periphery to study how human language encodes contextual information in the structure.

My broader theoretical interests are in the working of the computational mechanisms and devises of Grammar, which would answer questions in the understanding of the language faculty of human "mind/brain".

I joined IIT Delhi in 2024 as a doctoral researcher with Deepak Alok as my supervisor in the Department of Humanities and Social Sciences. My dissertation investigates the syntax of discourse in Karbi Hills (a Trans-Himalayan language) and Assamese (an Indo-Aryan language), spoken primarily in North-East India.
